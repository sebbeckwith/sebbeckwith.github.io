# 2. THE BIGGER POSITIONING SHIFT

The current homepage is still structured too much around:

> traditional surveys are bad, Gobby is better

We now want Gobby to feel like a distinct survey model, not just a substitute for an incumbent tool.

The visitor should understand early:

> Gobby has rethought how surveys work.

The conceptual territory is:

# WE’VE REINVENTED THE SURVEY

This does NOT mean making an unsupported claim that Gobby invented surveying or is objectively revolutionary.

It means Gobby should confidently own a different workflow.

The new product model should be understood as three primary points of difference:

## 1. OPEN QUESTIONS + ANSWERS

People can answer in their own words rather than only selecting from predefined choices.

Important:
this is not just “open questions”.

The word “answers” matters.

The value is that the organisation asking the question does not have to decide all possible answers in advance.

## 2. PEER ANALYSIS

Gobby can surface relevant anonymised responses while participants respond.

People can recognise, agree with or validate experiences that resonate.

This helps identify shared meaning across a group.

This is a major differentiator.

Do not describe it as simple popularity voting.

Relevant language:
- peer analysis
- shared experiences
- relevant responses
- collective insight
- what resonates across the group

## 3. THEMATIC ANALYSIS / CODING

Gobby supports organisations in:
- coding qualitative responses
- organising responses into themes
- reviewing evidence behind themes
- understanding patterns
- comparing different demographic or participant groups
- reporting findings

AI sits INSIDE this part of the workflow.

AI is not one of the top three standalone product pillars.

Correct hierarchy:

OPEN QUESTIONS + ANSWERS
→
PEER ANALYSIS
→
THEMATIC ANALYSIS

with:

AI assistance as an optional support layer within thematic analysis.

# 9. HOW GOBBY WORKS

Keep the current black editorial section.

Current workflow:

01 Ask openly
02 Hear real words
03 Find shared experience
04 Develop themes
05 Act and measure

This general logic is good.

However, update the language to reflect the new product hierarchy.

Recommended structure:

01
ASK OPEN QUESTIONS

Start with a question that leaves room for answers you did not anticipate.

02
HEAR REAL ANSWERS

People respond in their own words, giving context that tick boxes may miss.

03
ANALYSE WITH PEERS

Relevant anonymised responses can be surfaced so people can show what reflects their experience.

04
CODE & DEVELOP THEMES

Organise responses into meaningful codes and themes. Use optional AI assistance where useful, then review the original evidence.

05
COMPARE GROUPS

Explore how themes appear across demographics or participant groups.

06
REPORT & ACT

Turn findings into clear reporting, communicate what happens next and return later to measure change.

Consider whether six steps is too many visually.

If it makes the section too long, combine 05 and 06 appropriately.

Do not flatten the process into generic “collect → analyse → report” language.

The distinctive peer-analysis stage must remain visible.
